      The lookup table

      Atomic number Z, and the Z-th prime that stands in for it.

      Why it can't go wrong: the fundamental theorem of arithmetic says every whole number above 1 breaks into primes in exactly one way. Pin each element to its own prime and a formula becomes a number that can always be read back — the trick Kurt Gödel used in 1931 to make arithmetic talk about itself. The numbers grow fast: one caffeine molecule needs twenty digits.
